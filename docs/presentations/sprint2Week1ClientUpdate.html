Making an account

Making an account works from start to end

A new person makes an account with an email address and a strong password: at least fifteen characters with a capital, a small letter, a digit and a symbol.

  1. The form, with a clear message for every mistake
  2. The server saves each account once, with the password stored scrambled (hashed)
  3. A browser test makes an account the way a new person does

8 of 10 parts done, 1 in review. Next: logging in.

Make an account, the built page
Nick: making an account is the first thing a new person does. It works from start to end on our shared version: the form, the server that saves the account with the password hashed, and a browser test that makes an account the way a new person would. This is the real page, not the design. Logging in is what I am building now.

Profiles

From an empty profile to another member's page

Built as 10 changes that each wait for a teammate's check. Every name here is made up, as you asked on 24 September: only made-up test data.

Wesley: these pages are built and wait for a teammate's check. A young person creates a profile with a first name, an age band, a region and, if they want, a short line about themselves. On My profile they pick skills and interests from twenty suggestions or add their own. Other members see the first name, age band, region and tags. Until logging in is there, the pages use one fixed test account, and the profile moves to the logged-in account after that. Every name you see is made up, as you asked.

Safe for young people

Four rules for who may see a profile

Only for members

Every member page asks a visitor to log in first.

A parent agrees under 16

The profile stays hidden until then. Hiding it and the banner are built. Asking for the parent's email address and sending the email come next.

We ask little

An age band instead of a birth date, a region instead of a street.

Tags, not secrets

Skills and interests only, nothing about health, faith or other private matters.

Wesley: four rules decide who may see a profile. A member page is only for logged-in members. A profile under 16 stays hidden until a parent agrees. Hiding it and the banner are built. Asking for a parent's email address and sending the email to the parent come next. We ask little: never a birth date, a school or a street. And tags are only skills and interests. Kelvin is working on the list of all personal data in Connect and its risks.
