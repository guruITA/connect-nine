## Level 3, full-stack

**AA5 Adapting communication to the target group**
- What I made: the same progress the board shows, told for a client who is not technical: the real pages instead of code, "built and waiting for a teammate's check" instead of merge requests, points of work instead of weight, and only made-up members, as the client asked on 24 September 2026 (docs/client/clientQuestions.md, question 3). The questions for him are written as he would ask them himself, with the number of each question in docs/client/clientQuestions.md under it.
- Alternative I did not choose: showing the GitLab board itself. It holds the right numbers, but in words the client does not use, and it mixes the school cards with the work for Connect.

**MS1 Seeing risks early and keeping the work transferable**
- What I made: the numbers on the slides come from the board the evening before, with built work kept apart from closed work, so the client sees the real state after one of three weeks: 0 of 32 Connect cards closed, 10 built and in review, 7 in progress and 15 not started, and 8 cards that have no owner yet (3 must, 5 should). The deck is in the repository, so the team and the coach can open what was shown.
- Alternative I did not choose: counting the work in an open merge request as done, the way the Sprint 1 review did at the end of its sprint. After one week that would show 10 of 32 cards as done while no teammate has checked any of them yet.

**MS5 Supporting others in their contribution to the team goal**
- What I made: the agenda proposes a part for every team member, the part they built, with speaker notes per slide, so each of us can present their own work: Bayon Eerhart as Product Owner the sprint, Nick Hoebe making an account, Gurpreet Singh the test site and what is left, Kelvin van Voorst the questions for the client, and I the profiles.
- Alternative I did not choose: presenting everything myself. Then the client hears one voice about the work of five people.
